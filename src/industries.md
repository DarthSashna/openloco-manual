# Industries

Industries are large buildings that produce and receive cargo. Primary 
industries require nothing and their production can fluctuate over time. 
Secondary and tertiary industries require cargo to produce cargo; their 
production depends on how much cargo is sent to them.

A full list of Vanilla industry chains is provided in the [industry 
chains appendix](industry-chains.md). The types of cargo that an 
individual industry requires and produces requires can be seen in the 
tooltip one gets from hovering over it and at the bootom of the leftmost 
tab of its window.

## Primary Industries

Primary industries require will always send cargo to 
[stations](stations.md) whose catchment includes at least one tile of 
the industry building itself. They come in two types: extractive 
industries and agricultural industries. Coal mines and oil wells are 
examples of extractive industries. Chemical works also behave similarly 
to extractive industries. Farms and managed forests are examples of 
agricultural industries.

The production of extractive industries vary over time. However, they 
will generally increase their production if they have frequent services 
that transport goods from them. However, if transport falters, 
production is likely to decrease.

Agricultural industries produce cargo depending on the number of fields 
surrounding them. Managed forests can produce wood even when the forest 
tiles are snowed over. However, farms and vineyards cannot produce 
anything from fields that are snowed over or covered in trees. 
Agricultural industires can add new fields from time to time in nearby 
open areas.

> [!CAUTION]
>
> Agricultural industries are slow to replant fields that have been 
> demolished: Plan ahead to avoid destroying more fields than needed 
> when building near these industries. As [towns](towns.md) grow, they 
> may build over fields.

## Secondary and Tertiary Industries

Secondary and tertiary industries require cargo be received from nearby 
stations to produce cargo of their own. Some of these accept only one 
type of cargo. For example, paper mills accept only wood. Others can 
produce from multiple types of cargo. For example, food processing 
plants can produce food from either livestock or grain.

The steel mill and power station are unique. The steel mill requires 
both coal and iron ore to be regularly delivered before it will produce 
steel. The power station accepts coal and never produces anything.

## Industry Opening and Closure

Each scenario starts with some industries. Addtionally, new industries 
can open over time. However, industries may also close down. There is 
nothing that one can do to prevent this from occuring.

## Viewing Industry Details

Hovering the cursor over an industry will show a tooltip. This tooltip 
gives the industry's name and what cargoes it accepts and produces. 
Clicking on the industry building will show a window with three tabs. 
The left tab shows a viewport of the industry. The middle tab shows its 
historical production and the right tab shows how much cargo was 
delivered and produced last month.

## Viewing and Funding Industries

The industries window is accessible from the towns and industries menu 
in the top-right corner of the viewport. It can also be opened by 
right-clicking an industry. The left tab lists industries and the right 
tab lets one fund new secondary and tertiary industries.

The industry list in the left tab can be sorted by name, status, the 
fraction of cargo transported and how much cargo was produced last 
month. Clicking on an industry's entry on the list will open the window 
for that industry.

To fund an industry, select its preview from the right tab. Then, move 
the cursor over the part of the game world you want build it. If it can 
be funded there, you will see a ghost of the industry. Click to fund it.

Industries cannot neither be built over water nor funded too close to 
another industry. Funding industries is also expensive. Primary 
industries can never be funded. Certain secondary and tertiary 
industries also cannot be funded.
