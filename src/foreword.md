# Foreword

<cite>Chris Sawyer's Locomotion</cite> was published by Atari in 2004 
for Windows (MobyGames n.d.). Critics panned it for a failure to improve 
on <cite>Transport Tycoon</cite> and <cite>Transport Tycoon 
Deluxe</cite>, including in graphics and pathfinding, and an 
inappropriate construction system and interface inherited from 
<cite>RollerCoaster Tycoon</cite> (Adams 2018) (Rausch 2004) (Ocampo 
2004). Most fans of the original game kept playing it or moved onto 
<cite>OpenTTD</cite>, a free and open-source extended clone (OpenTTD 
developers n.d.).

However, there are people who do enjoy <cite>Locomotion</cite>. These 
people have created let's plays and custom scenarios featuring custom 
vehicles and industries ('matsku84' 2015-17). In 2018, work began to 
create a free and open source game called <cite>OpenLoco</cite>. It 
copies <cite>Locomotion</cite>'s gameplay and can use its data formats 
(OpenLoco developers, c. 2025). While it does not rely on the original 
executable, as of February 2026, the game cannot be realistically played 
without the original <cite>Locomotion</cite> graphics.

Already, <cite>OpenLoco</cite> has major improvements over 
<cite>Locomotion</cite>. Vehicle breakdowns and the AI competitors, 
which are two of the original game's most disliked features, can be 
disabled. It is now possible to build long segments of straight track by 
holding the build piece button. Additionally, track and stations can be 
built using a click-and-drag feature. Vehicles can also be cloned from a 
menu.

The main focus of current development is on creating new object and save 
formats that extend the limitations of the original formats. This will 
allow for larger maps and more expansive vehicle and industry sets. It 
will also allow for deeper extensions to gameplay, such as path-based 
signalling and improved vehicle paths.
